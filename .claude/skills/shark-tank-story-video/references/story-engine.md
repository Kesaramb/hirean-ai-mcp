# The GAG Story Engine — editorial reference

A repeatable system for turning any Shark Tank company into a story a total stranger will
stop for, click, and finish. Three viewer decisions, one layer each: **stop** (thumbnail),
**click** (title completes the thumbnail's question), **stay** (the story re-earns attention
every 30 seconds).

Evidence base (GAG channel, Search Console + Studio, Jul–Aug 2026): conflict-framed titles
clicked **2.4×** better than neutral ones (0.87% vs 0.37% CTR); catalog language
("Update: Net Worth, Sales &…") clicked ~3× worse than everything else (0.19%). Twelve
well-made videos sat dead on 33,000 impressions/week because the package was dead.

## Layer 0 — The prime rule

**Package first. Script second. Film/edit last.** Choose the entity, run the Stranger Test,
lock title + thumbnail as one unit — only if the package survives do you write the story.
If you can't package it, don't produce it.

### The Stranger Test (the gate every idea must pass)

Describe the video in one sentence to someone who has **never seen Shark Tank**, using no
context they'd need explained. If the sentence still creates an itch, it passes.

- ✅ "A company signed a $2.5M deal on national TV, and within a year the whole thing had
  collapsed." — no prerequisite knowledge; money + mystery are universal.
- ❌ "An update on what happened to iCapsulate after Season 3 of Shark Tank Australia." —
  requires the viewer to already care. That's an encyclopedia entry, not a story.

## Layer 1 — The five story shapes

Every aftermath is one of five plots. Pick the shape before writing a word — the shape decides
the cold open, the midpoint turn, and the verdict.

| # | Shape | Arc | How it opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Collapse** | rise → triumph → hidden rot → fall | Open at the wreckage, rewind to the glory. (Proven: iCapsulate, XCraft, Breathometer) |
| 2 | **The Vindication** | rejected → mocked → grind → massive win | Open at the win, rewind to the humiliation. (Proven: Jamie Siminoff / Ring, 2.15% CTR; Coffee Meets Bagel's rejected $30M) |
| 3 | **The Copycat War** | giant vs upstart → escalation → verdict | Two players, one market, open hostility — conflict is pre-installed; you referee it. (Proven: Scrubbie vs Scrub Daddy, 0.97% CTR) |
| 4 | **The Disappearance** | deal → silence → investigation → answer | They took the money and vanished from the record. You investigate on camera; the receipts are the plot. |
| 5 | **The Devil's Bargain** | dream deal → fine print → the cost | The deal closed — and the deal itself was the trap. Royalties, control, dilution. The villain is the contract. |

**Choosing:** ask *"what is the gap between what the TV moment promised and what actually
happened?"* That gap **is** the story. **No gap → no video.** This kills "success update"
videos before they waste a week.

## Layer 2 — The 8-beat spine (GAG Story Circle)

Dan Harmon's loop, rebuilt for money documentaries. Timings assume an 8-minute video
(mid-roll eligible). For 12+ minutes, stretch THE RECEIPTS — never THE HUMAN.

| Time | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | **THE WOUND** | Open at the moment of maximum consequence — the wreckage, the number, the silence. State the question the whole video answers; promise the answer is coming. No intro, no logo. *(Harmon's comfort zone, inverted: comfort already died.)* |
| 0:20–1:00 | **THE HUMAN** | Who they were before, in three sentences. One humanizing, specific detail (the garage, the day job, the 4am shifts). The viewer must briefly *like* them — consequence only lands on someone we know. *(You / Need.)* |
| 1:00–2:30 | **THE TANK** | The pitch as a scene, not a summary: the ask, the counters, the handshake — played as drama with the actual moments. Network TV already filmed your second act; use it. *(Go.)* |
| 2:30–3:30 | **THE HONEYMOON** | Everything works — and you plant the seed of doom: one visible detail the audience can see is a problem before the founder does. Dramatic irony is retention glue. *(Search.)* |
| 3:30–4:00 | **THE TURN** | The single sentence where it breaks. One "but", never "and then". The shape decides it: the deal quietly never closed · the giant noticed · the royalty came due · the socials went dark. *(Find — the midpoint.)* |
| 4:00–6:00 | **THE RECEIPTS** | Escalate with evidence on screen: filings, archived pages, trademark records, revenue numbers — each shown, each sourced. Verification played as spectacle, stakes rising each time. This is the moat: nobody else in the niche shows the receipts. *(Take — pay a heavy price.)* |
| 6:00–7:00 | **THE VERDICT** | Answer the opening question with a number. Replay the opening image — now the viewer understands it. The verdict must be concrete: a figure, a status, a fate. *(Return.)* |
| 7:00–7:30 | **THE LESSON + LOOP** | 15 seconds of meaning — one transferable lesson (the satisfaction signal the 2026 algorithm weights above watch time). Then throw the next hook: "[Next company] made the exact opposite bet — and it worked", with the end screen. Every ending is the next story's cold open. *(Change — the circle restarts.)* |

## Layer 3 — The six retention laws

1. **But/Therefore, never And-Then.** Read the script aloud: every beat must connect to the
   next with "but" or "therefore". Any "and then" is a cut. (The South Park writers' room rule.)
2. **The loop ledger never hits zero.** Open a new question before closing the current one.
   At any moment before the final 30 seconds, at least one question must be open.
3. **The 30-second contract.** Every 30 seconds delivers a reveal, a number, or a reversal.
   A block with none gets cut — it's where retention graphs die.
4. **Cold open, always.** No intro, no branding, no "in this video". The first frame is already
   the story. Channel identity lives in the voice and the receipts, not a logo sting.
5. **Numbers appear on screen, with the source visible.** Every claim carries its receipt —
   simultaneously the differentiation, the legal safety, and the trust/satisfaction signal the
   algorithm formally rewards.
6. **Scenes beat narration.** Show the Tank moment; don't describe it. Where no footage exists,
   show the artifact (the filing, the archived site, the delisted product page) while the voice tells it.

## The canvas — one page per video

Fill before scripting; any empty box = the video isn't ready. (Fillable version:
`resources/canvas-template.md`.)

1. **Entity** — company + founder. One story per video.
2. **Shape** — pick one of the five.
3. **The Gap** — TV promise vs reality. No gap = no video.
4. **Stranger sentence** — one sentence, zero prerequisite context, creates an itch.
5. **The package** — thumbnail = the emotion/object; title = the unresolved half of the same
   question. Never repeat each other; never resolve.
6. **Cold-open moment** — the single image/number/sentence of maximum consequence (Beat 1).
7. **The But** — the one-sentence midpoint turn. If you can't write it, the shape is wrong.
8. **Three receipts** — three verifiable, on-screen pieces of evidence + sources. ⚑ flag anything unverified.
9. **The verdict number** — the concrete answer to the opening question.
10. **Lesson + handoff** — one transferable takeaway + which story the end screen throws to.

## Package rules & rehab

Pattern in every successful rehab: **remove the words that resolve** ("Update", "Success",
"Saved", "Why the…"), **keep a human subject**, **end on an open door**.

| Before (encyclopedia entry) | After (engine version) |
|---|---|
| "BootayBag Shark Tank Update: Success, Sales & Net Worth" (0.00% CTR) | Shape 4: "She Turned Down the Sharks' Money. Then Her Company Went Silent." |
| "How Tree T-Pee Saved Farmers Millions After Shark Tank" (0.17%) | Shape 2: "He Refused to Raise His Price. It Made Him Unforgettable." |
| "XCraft After Shark Tank: Why the $1.5M Deal Fell Apart" (0.14%) | Shape 5: "Five Sharks Said Yes. The Deal Died Anyway. Nobody Reported Why." |

A title that resolves the story kills the click even when it performs "well": iCapsulate's
"collapses after biggest deal ever made" was the channel's best (0.82% CTR) yet still gave the
verdict away; the engine version withholds it — *"The Biggest Deal in Shark Tank History Never
Actually Happened."*

## Worked example — iCapsulate, Shape 1: The Collapse

- **Gap:** biggest deal in the show's history → company in liquidation within ~a year ⚑
- **Stranger sentence:** "A coffee company landed the biggest investment in the show's history
  on national TV — and it never saw most of the money."
- **Package:** thumbnail = the handshake with a red "IN LIQUIDATION" stamp + "≈$2.5M" ⚑;
  title = "The Biggest Deal in Shark Tank History Never Actually Happened".
- **Cold open (0:00):** "This handshake was worth two and a half million dollars. It's the most
  money ever offered on Australian Shark Tank. [beat] Almost none of it was ever paid. Here's
  what the cameras didn't show."
- **The But:** "The deal was announced on TV — **but** TV deals aren't contracts. Due diligence
  started after the cameras stopped."
- **Receipts:** liquidation filing · due-diligence collapse reporting · founder's post-show statements.
- **Verdict:** the status today + what investors and suppliers actually recovered.
- **Lesson + handoff:** "A deal on TV is an offer, not a wire transfer — roughly half of them
  quietly die in diligence." → throws to XCraft ("this one fell apart for the opposite reason").

## Verification rule (absolute)

Every `⚑ VERIFY` must be resolved against a primary source before publishing. **Never ship an
unverified claim about a company's status or finances.** Unresolved flags are surfaced to the
user at delivery as a publish blocker.
